# Introduction Profile

## Purpose

Construct the paper-level need for the contribution. Introduce the technique
only after the reader understands what capability a successful solution needs.

## Choose one primary argument

### Study-driven

Use the **study-driven** structure when an empirical investigation establishes
the design space or motivates the technique:

```text
problem background
→ existing solutions
→ unknown capability or unresolved question
→ empirical study
→ findings
→ design requirements
→ technique
→ evaluation and contributions
```

### Technique-driven

Use the **technique-driven** structure when a concrete limitation leads to a
new method:

```text
problem background
→ solution landscape
→ limitation of the relevant solution
→ root cause
→ required capability
→ key insight
→ technique
→ evaluation and contributions
```

Do not mix the two structures merely to mention a study and a technique. Choose
the chain that carries the paper's central argument.

## Required bridge

Before writing “To this end, we propose ...”, establish:

1. why existing solutions are insufficient;
2. the mechanism or missing capability behind that insufficiency;
3. what a successful solution must provide.

The method should answer the required capability, not merely follow the
surface limitation.

## Audit

- Does the background narrow toward the exact problem?
- Is the relevant solution landscape described before criticism?
- Does every challenge derive from the root cause or study findings?
- Do contributions correspond to the established needs?
- Are evaluation summaries bounded by actual results?
